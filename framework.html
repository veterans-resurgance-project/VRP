Strategic Collaboration Framework

In-Kind Work & Managing the Three Operations Streams to protect federal grant eligibility, maintain BOLI/FLSA compliance, and ensure clean accounting.

1. Framing In-Kind Work (Space, Storage, Equipment)

trainingMatching In-Kind Contribution or a Mutual Benefit Exchange in your cooperative agreements.

The Problem with Framing it as Training Hours

training

The Correct Valuation Method:

  • The Partner's Contribution: The logging company or landlord documents the fair market rental value of the shop space, yard, or equipment (e.g., "$1,200/month shop space"). This counts as their official "Non-Federal In-Kind Match," which is highly beneficial for satisfying federal grant matching requirements.
  • The Project's Contribution: The non-profit provides an organized, disciplined cohort to execute environmental stabilization or fuel reduction on designated public/private training grounds under an MOU. The crew's activities are strictly billed as Vocational Practicum / Field Training Hours towards their certification, independent of real estate valuation.

2. The Three Operational Streams Structured

To keep your funding legal and scalable, the three streams exist as distinct financial and operational channels:

Stream 1: The Training/Classroom Stream (Non-Profit VRP) Funded entirely by federal/state grants and philanthropic donations. Veterans receive a flat stipend, free technical training (sawyer certifications, drone mapping operations), and personal safety gear (PPE). Zero commercial timber is harvested or sold during this stream.
Stream 2: The Free Community Work Stream (Non-Profit VRP) Funded under the non-profit's operational umbrella as community service and field conditioning. Covers the 16-to-24 hour free brush-clearing safety windows for local properties, acting as live-ground training and building massive community trust.
Stream 3: The Paid Commercial Contract Stream (Cascadia LLC) Funded directly by market-rate commercial subcontracts. Once a job crosses the complexity threshold or acreage limit, Cascadia Forestry Solutions LLC takes over, transitioning veterans to an hourly wage of $24.50/hr for up to 250 hours.

3. The "Up-Skilling Loop" & Milestone Tracking

Veterans can return to the training stream to upgrade their skills after starting paid contract work, structured as a cyclical loop paired with precise tracking benchmarks.

  • The Mechanics of the Return Loop: When a veteran finishes their 250-hour commercial contract limit, they hit an administrative gate. If not immediately picked up for permanent full-time employment by a private logging partner, they step back down into the training stream to add a new specialty (e.g., transitioning from a Ground Sawyer to an Advanced UAV Mapping Pilot).
  • The Financial Shift: While back in training for the new specialty, their hourly contract wage ends, and they transition back onto the grant-sponsored training stipend, preventing double-dipping.
  • Preventing "Forever Trainees": To remain compliant with labor laws and grant rules, a participant cannot lounge in the training stream indefinitely; their return must be strictly tied to a new, higher-tier training track with distinct curriculum limits.
