Career Pathways & Curriculum

Training, Certifications & Advancement

Explore the complete field operations and professional advancement pathway for our Linn County initiatives. Review the mandatory safety baseline, choose your specialized operational track, and map out your path to leadership.

Phase 1

Initial Onboarding: Basic Safety Baseline

Required for all crew members before stepping onto any active work site or hill operation.

OSHA 10-Hour General Industry or Construction Safety

Foundational instruction covering hazard recognition, slips/trips/falls, electrical safety, and personal protective equipment (PPE) standards.

First Aid, CPR, and AED Certification

Standardized multi-hour certification ensuring every crew member can respond immediately to medical emergencies, trauma, and environmental injuries in remote settings.

Job Hazard Analysis (JHA) & Daily Briefings

Mandatory site-specific safety orientation teaching participants how to actively participate in morning tailgates, identify changing site risks, and enforce the "Stop Work Authority" protocol.

Basic PPE Compliance & Inspection

Hands-on training on fitting, inspecting, and maintaining mandatory site gear, including ANSI-rated eye and ear protection, leather gloves, hard hats, and logger boots.

Phase 2

Operational Specialization Tracks

Spring / Summer

Track 1: Wildland Fire & Forestry Mitigation

Target Certification: NWCG Firefighter Type 2 (FFT2) & Ground Sawyer

  • IS-100: Introduction to the Incident Command System (FEMA online self-study, ~3 hours).
  • S-190: Introduction to Wildland Fire Behavior (Theory covering fire triangle, weather, fuels, and topography, ~6–8 hours).
  • S-130: Firefighter Training (Comprehensive practical instruction on fireline construction, tool handling, and suppression tactics; includes a mandatory supervised field day, ~30–35 hours).
  • L-180: Human Factors on the Fireline (Situational awareness, team communication, and human performance under stress).
  • Arduous Work Capacity Test ("Pack Test"): Agency-administered physical evaluation requiring a 3-mile walk with a 45-pound pack in 45 minutes or less.
  • NWCG Chainsaw Safety Standard Module: Practical field training covering safe felling, limbing under tension, bucking, and reacting to reactive forces.
Winter / Technical

Track 2: Aerial Drone Mapping & GIS

Target Certification: FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate

  • Aeronautical Knowledge & Regulations: Title 14 CFR Part 107 rules, small unmanned aircraft system (sUAS) limits, and operational compliance.
  • Airspace Classification & Operating Requirements: Reading sectional charts, understanding controlled/uncontrolled airspace, and processing LAANC or manual waivers.
  • Aviation Weather & Sources: Analyzing METARs, TAFs, wind factors, and microclimate effects on flight telemetry.
  • Flight Planning & Telemetry Programming: Ground-control software training to set automated grid flight paths for RGB and thermal sensors.
  • Post-Flight Data Processing & GIS: Offline data stitching, orthomosaic generation, and post-wildfire burn-scar topological evaluation.
Winter / Controlled Environment

Track 3: Greenhouse & Native Nursery Propagation

Target Certification: Ecological Restoration & Nursery Technician Credential

  • Seed Science & Processing: Protocols for ethical wild-seed collection, cleaning, scarification, and cold stratification of native conifer and riparian species.
  • Soil Science & Mycorrhizal Inoculation: Preparing growing media, balancing soil biology, and optimizing nutrient delivery for native starts.
  • Controlled-Environment Microclimate Management: Monitoring and automating hoop-house humidity, temperature, photoperiods, and drip-line irrigation systems.
  • Inventory & Operations Management: Tracking batch survival rates, maintaining nursery logs, and managing facility upkeep.
Phase 3

Leadership Progression & Supervisory Ranks

Level 1

Transition to Crew Leader / Squad Boss (FFT1)

Prerequisites: Fully qualified FFT2 and demonstrated field proficiency.

  • ICS-200: ICS for Single Resources and Initial Action Incidents (Intermediate Incident Command System structure).
  • S-290: Intermediate Wildland Fire Behavior (Detailed analysis of environmental factors affecting fire behavior, topographic influences, and predictive fuel models).
  • L-280: Followership to Leadership (Transitioning from team member to front-line supervisor, emphasizing operational leadership, decision-making, and ethical responsibilities).
  • Practical Evaluation: Completion of the official FFT1 Position Task Book (PTB), requiring documented performance supervising a small team during live field exercises or active fire suppression operations.
Level 2

Progression to Site Leader / Single Resource Boss (CRWB / ENGB)

Prerequisites: Fully qualified FFT1 status and successful completion of Level 1 modules.

  • S-230: Crew Boss (Single Resource) (Blended online and classroom course covering tactical decision-making, risk management, mobilization, entrapment avoidance, and operational leadership).
  • S-260: Interagency Incident Business Management (Administrative protocols, time recording, procurement, and property management on incidents).
  • S-270: Basic Air Operations (Coordinating and communicating safely with helicopters and fixed-wing air tankers).
  • Practical Evaluation: Completion of the rigorous Single Resource Boss Position Task Book (CRWB/ENGB), verified by a qualified evaluator during commercial or community project deployments, leading to formal agency credentialing.
